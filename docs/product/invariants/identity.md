# Invariant register — Identity, steering, and work

[Invariant register](../invariants.md) · [Spice product model](../README.md)
## Identity and authority

| Label | Invariant |
| --- | --- |
| <a id="identity-a-task-s-born-stamp-is-its-only-stored-identity"></a>**A task's `born` stamp is its only stored identity** | The rendered handle derives a key from the project's rightmost segment and is never stored, so re-homing renames for free. Every surface resolves a handle by its stamp alone: a handle rendered under another project names the same task, the stem never grants or narrows visibility, and answers render the current handle. Stamp order is not creation chronology; journal sequence and durable queue origin are. |
| <a id="identity-an-occupant-address-is-its-birth-position"></a>**An occupant address is its birth position** | An occupant's only peer address is the journal position of the one `lane::started` fact recording its birth. It is derived from that fact, never minted or stored, so occupants started in the same instant keep distinct addresses. Proof reads exactly the addressed position and refuses rather than picking when no birth, a non-birth fact, or a repeated birth is found. |
| <a id="identity-an-address-executes-only-for-its-seated-occupant"></a>**An address executes only for its seated occupant** | An address resolves only while the residency seats that exact occupant on its birth lane; it never follows the lane to a successor. Resident restart adoption keeps the occupant and its address. Every wake, resume, import, and renewal seats a different occupant with its own address, and the predecessor's address expires. What an address already published is [bound to its lane](#steering-an-authored-msg-packet-admits-each-proved-recipient), not to the address. |
| <a id="identity-a-discovered-seat-address-resolves-to-the-occupant-shown"></a>**A discovered seat address resolves to the occupant shown** | The session briefing names the reader's own address and its teammates' addresses, and `spice lane list` shows each lane's address. Only a seated occupant whose birth is proved exactly once on that lane shows one, and it is the address the resolver executes; a vacant seat or unproved birth shows as unavailable, never as a guessed or predecessor address. |
| <a id="identity-every-fact-has-exactly-one-authority"></a>**Every fact has exactly one authority** | Every fact has exactly one authority. Consumers ask the owner rather than re-deriving from a cheaper nearby source. |
| <a id="identity-a-resume-cursor-belongs-to-one-exact-stream"></a>**A resume cursor belongs to one exact stream** | One authenticated stream carries an authoritative snapshot followed by accepted facts in monotone sequence within one resident epoch, applied exactly once in that order. A cursor resumes only its exact application generation and resident epoch; a mismatch or gap requires a fresh snapshot rather than a guessed merge. |
| <a id="identity-durable-facts-and-heads-remain-distinct-from-rebuildable-index-rows"></a>**Durable facts and heads remain distinct from rebuildable index rows** | A derived index may share the authority store and transaction, but rebuilding it never mutates immutable facts or heads. |
| <a id="identity-a-projection-family-must-declare-its-source"></a>**A projection family must declare its source** | A projection family declares its source, its progress record, its replay horizon, its refill, and what it can still say when the source no longer reaches back. |
| <a id="identity-room-relevance-is-shadow-evidence-before-automation"></a>**Room relevance is shadow evidence before automation** | A room relevance observation is an append-only Spice fact naming its anchor, a journal progress horizon, one to eight exact available or explicitly unavailable sources, a finite classification, and its explanation, exceptions, and uncertainty. Only a relevant contribution can declare progress; declines, duplicates, stale activity, missed opportunities, agreement, message count, silence, and elapsed time cannot. After each commit the resident observes every settled [question result](#identity-room-questions-separate-delivery-from-result-authority) asked in a task room exactly once, under the result's own identity, at the result head's position, citing the ASK's fact and the fact that answered or closed it: an answer is unknown with unknown progress, because the settlement it cites cannot tell evidence from agreement, a decline is declined, a timeout is a missed opportunity, and a cancellation is stale activity, none of them progress; a result asked outside any room is never observed. Only the operator assesses an observation, once, with `spice room assess OBSERVATION confirmed|false-positive|unknown TEXT`. `spice room relevance HANDLE` and `spice://task/HANDLE/room/relevance` page a room's observations whole in identity order with their sources and assessments, beside whole-room classification, progress, and assessment counts. Observing, assessing, and reading change no delivery, participant, result, task, claim, group, view, review, or authorization authority. |
| <a id="identity-a-design-notice-is-background-awareness-once-per-claim"></a>**A Design notice is background awareness, once per claim** | After each commit that changes task, claim, or residency authority, the resident compares every occupied lane's active claim with every open Design: one whose intent is Design and whose status is pending with no completion. A statement's terms are its lowercase words: text splits on every character that is not a letter or digit, a run holding a digit is inert, words under four letters, words whose lowercase form is not wholly lowercase letters, and common function words drop, and one trailing `s` after a stem of four or more letters folds away. A Design's terms come from its title and description; the claimed task's from its title, description, and acceptance. The task matches when they share at least three distinct terms, at least one of them from the Design's title. The claimed task itself, a Design the lane holds or created, and a Design whose room the lane already listens to for any reason are never told. The resident commits one Spice-owned `room::noticed` record (lane, occupant, claimed task, the claim's winning fact, the Design, and up to twelve matched terms in ascending order) before it hands the exact occupant one private background notice with the Design's handle, title, first-sentence pitch, matched terms, `spice room history HANDLE`, and a `MSG #HANDLE` contribution. A Design is told to a lane once per claim, because a later pass skips every record already naming that lane, claim, and Design. The record is no room publication: it adds no attention or participation reason, opens no steering, needs no acknowledgment, starts no work, and wakes no lane. `spice room awareness HANDLE` (`spice://task/HANDLE/room/awareness`) pages every record of one Design in journal order. |
| <a id="identity-an-operator-room-post-records-before-any-listener-catch-up"></a>**An operator room post records before any listener catch-up** | `spice room post HANDLE TEXT` resolves the current task handle to its born stamp and commits the complete text once under the command identity. Its reply reports every current listener's occupied, vacant, unavailable, or unknown topology state; a vacant listener waits for its next start, while a room with no current listener offers explicit Start/direction. Recording creates no participant acknowledgment, claim, wake, interruption, or lane participation. Only a room whose anchor is unresolved takes a new post: a completed, closed, or deleted anchor refuses with `room::anchor-settled` naming its standing and writes nothing, and the browser composer of a settled Design names that standing and offers no post. A settled room's history stays readable, and receipts and question results already open in it still settle. |
| <a id="identity-room-coordination-reuses-native-steering-and-possession"></a>**Room coordination reuses native steering and possession** | `spice room start` and `spice room resume` commit one scoped operator direction and an immutable room marker together. `ADDRESS` must name the current address of an occupied seat; a simulated, degraded, or other unoccupied seat refuses before submission with its address and disposition, so no direction or marker is written. The request names the unresolved criterion, bounded contribution, and stop condition; its recipient acquires the anchor or exact related preparation only through ordinary native task possession. When that recipient does not hold the anchor, the direction names `spice task capture HANDLE`, the exact native command that claims it without relying on allocator selection. The same transaction rechecks that the target lane holds no active claim other than this anchor; a concurrent win on another task refuses the coordination without publishing its direction or marker. Filing visibility continues to keep hidden controller anchors out of automatic allocation. `spice room put-aside` cancels only unanswered room coordination and, when the anchor is held, sends its holder a checkpoint/free direction without freeing the claim. The journal admits each marker only from the operator, directly after the same write's directions it names: a start's room coordination direction, a put-aside's cancellations of that room's coordination directions, exactly and in order, and its stop to the lane holding the active claim win it cites; commit, replay, and verification refuse every other shape. Coordination state reads from each room's latest markers alone, so it stays operable however much history the room holds; each coordination write is guarded on those markers, so at most one request is live. The stop projection remains visible for that exact claim win until ordinary completion, free, or lapse changes native possession; the reply asks the initiating browser to hide the room without storing browser state. |
| <a id="identity-an-operator-rule-bounds-automated-room-resumes"></a>**An operator rule bounds automated room resumes** | `spice room start` or `spice room resume` with `--auto-resume N` records on its room marker the operator's rule for up to N (1 through 8) automated resumes, to the same target and scope. While the room's latest marker carries that rule with budget left and nothing has put the room aside since, the resident resumes exactly when the target acknowledged the latest request, the anchor is pending and unclaimed, and a room publication by another party follows both that acknowledgment and the target's latest room publication. The resume runs the operator Resume's own staging, guards, and refusals and writes only its direction and marker; the marker names the authorizing marker, its step, and that publication. A pending request, silence, agreement with no new input, the target's own publications, a decline, a lapse, a cancellation, an unseated target, a settled or claimed anchor, and a spent budget each write nothing, so an unchanged cycle stops. A Start or Resume without the flag ends the rule, and `spice room status` shows the live rule's limit and steps spent. The journal admits an automated marker only as the next step of its room's latest rule, within its limit, after no put-aside, citing a publication in that room. |
| <a id="identity-a-room-reads-back-from-its-owning-sources"></a>**A room reads back from its owning sources** | `spice room history HANDLE` and `spice://task/HANDLE/room` resolve the handle to its born stamp, so any spelling a re-homed task has carried reads the same room under its current handle, and page its publications oldest first. Each row names its sender, its author occupant for an agent packet, and every directed route with that directive's current steering receipt; its text comes from the authority that owns it, the journal fact for an operator post or operator answer and the author's tape for a packet. A packet or operator answer that conversed also names its verb, its operator item receipt, and its question result at their current heads, each apart from the room record itself. Coordination publications occupy that same order and link the journal fact that owns their marker. A source that no longer yields the bytes renders unavailable with its reason, never as empty text. Publications are never cut: limits count whole publications, and a withheld page continues at the first withheld one, so following continuations visits each once. A malformed or unknown handle, or a cursor naming no publication of this room, refuses. Reading joins, acknowledges, delivers, and wakes nothing. |
| <a id="identity-room-questions-separate-delivery-from-result-authority"></a>**Room questions separate delivery from result authority** | Every selector freezes its own typed operator, lane, or task destination and optional task context at admission. A lane's operator route may instead name the key of an operator direction the operator sent that lane; admission, replay, and verification refuse one off the operator destination, unknown, addressed to another lane, or not from the operator, and the direction's text stays owned by its steering head. Each directed lane route of an MSG or ASK, and each answer returned to a lane requester, is exactly one native steering directive that the same write opened. A lane author's directive is a peer message told from its own record; an operator's directive is operator direction. Each goes to that lane in that route's context, and no room receipt head shadows it. Native steering owns that delivery's key, inbox, ACK and NAK, wake, lapse, recovery, dead letter, and renewal. An ASK's directive carries its public result handle as its key. Each operator-destination route and each answer returned to the operator opens one operator item: the publication stamp keys an MSG item, the result handle keys an ASK item, and the answer's stamp keys a return item. An item lapses only at or after its bounded expiry, and only the resident lapses it. An expired item has lapsed, never decided: only the operator recovers it, returning it to pending under the same key with a fresh bounded expiry. ACK settles only its delivery. NAK of a live ASK directive or ASK item also declines its pending result in the same fact, while a NAK after the result settled moves only the delivery. The result keeps immutable source, context, and deadline and one guarded pending-to-terminal head; no directive or item lapse, recovery, dead letter, or renewal changes, extends, or reopens it. ANS must match the stored respondent and context. In one write it settles the result, retires the still-live ASK directive or item (an ANS that leaves a live ASK directive unretired refuses), and opens the one nonrecursive return delivery to the stored requester. Content admission reserves bounded ACK/NAK capacity before accepting bytes, routes, or pending results, so saturation cannot partly settle an answer or strand a control return. The journal owns this authority as write-once publications with guarded operator item and result heads that replay rebuilds. A lane admits only its own transcript hunk, while the operator's text and clauses are owned by their own facts. Only the operator controls an item, only stored parties or the operator decline or cancel, and only the resident times out a result, at or after its deadline. Lateness is decided by the commit instant, so an ANS at or after the deadline refuses even before any timeout lands. Pending results are bounded per requester, while MSG, controls, and other requesters still admit. A fact that would move nothing, or whose first consequence refuses, writes nothing. |
| <a id="identity-room-conversation-controls-are-attributed-commands"></a>**Room conversation controls are attributed commands** | Only the operator runs `spice room ack|nak ITEM... --clause TEXT`, `spice room answer RESULT TEXT [--context HANDLE]`, `spice room recover ITEM`, and `spice room assess OBSERVATION VERDICT TEXT`; a lane settles its own deliveries with ACK/NAK prose. `spice room decline|cancel RESULT --clause TEXT` closes a pending [question result](#identity-room-questions-separate-delivery-from-result-authority) for its stored respondent or requester, and the operator closes for a stored lane party only with `--override`, which is refused when the operator is that party. Each control reads the heads it moves and guards their positions, so a raced control refuses `room::control-moved` and writes nothing; every refusal names the item or result state it observed and the command that repairs it. A multi-item control reports every item and question result it moved in input-item order. An operator answer is one write: the result settles, a still-pending ASK item settles, one operator direction returns `ANS RESULT: TEXT` to the requesting lane in the question's context, and the answer is published in that one room. The journal envelope's actor attributes every decision. `spice room inbox` and `spice://room/inbox` page every pending or lapsed operator item whole in key order, each with its publication text from its owning source and an ASK's result; `spice room result RESULT` and `spice://room/result/RESULT` read one result with its parties, deadline, state, its closer and override mark, and its answer's return receipt. These reads name recorded publications, participant receipts, and results apart, leave review coverage and execution authorization to `preparation show`, and change nothing. |
| <a id="identity-a-lane-leaves-a-room-by-an-attributed-act"></a>**A lane leaves a room by an attributed act** | Each room in a lane's catch-up names that lane's current listening reasons and `spice room leave HANDLE --clause TEXT`. Only a lane leaves, under its own actor, and the leave is recorded once as a `room::left` publication that room history and standing show with its clause; a repeated leave while one is in force records nothing. A leave in force ends listening derived from contribution, recent activity, and invitations it postdates, so later room publications reach that lane in no catch-up. The lane's next contribution or a later lane-addressed packet restores listening only for publications in the new listening interval; it never replays publications skipped while the leave held. Native work on the anchor or related tasks, a live coordination or stop request, and an owed receipt or question result keep the lane listening, and the leave reply names each. Leaving never frees a claim, settles a delivery, or answers a question. |
| <a id="identity-a-lane-contributes-typed-problem-space-records-to-an-open-design"></a>**A lane contributes typed problem-space records to an open Design** | `spice room contribute HANDLE KIND TEXT [--source SOURCE...]` appends one immutable `room::contributed` publication to an open Design's room: one whose intent is Design and whose workflow is pending with no completion. KIND is `reason-for`, `reason-against`, `evidence`, or `question`; the record names that kind, its lane author as actor, its time, its trimmed nonempty text of at most 4096 bytes, and zero to eight distinct exact sources, each spelled as its JSON `{"kind":"journal","fact":"SEQ"}`, `{"kind":"transcript",...}` hunk, or `{"kind":"artifact","digest":"DIGEST"}`. Only a lane contributes, under its own actor. Admission witnesses the Design's statement and workflow heads and every source at one snapshot: a journal fact at or before that snapshot, a transcript hunk the tape still reads, or an artifact retained on that Design. A missing, hidden, changed, unreadable, or unsupported source refuses `room::contribution-source-unavailable` naming it, and a concluded Design refuses `room::contribution-design-concluded`; the journal applies the same actor, bounds, source, and openness checks again at commit. `spice room history HANDLE` and `spice://task/HANDLE/room` keep every publication in one chronological page, render a contribution with its kind, text, and each source available or typed unavailable, never fabricated, and group the page's contributions by kind in journal order; `--contributions` (`?contributions`) pages only contributions. Untyped posts and packets stay conversation and are never reinterpreted. Writing or reading a contribution changes no task statement or workflow, claim, working group, candidate, decision, or output authorization. |
| <a id="identity-the-resident-settles-room-deadlines-once"></a>**The resident settles room deadlines once** | While a resident is seated, it arms every pending [question result](#identity-room-questions-separate-delivery-from-result-authority) at its immutable deadline and every pending operator item at its expiry, from durable heads alone, so a restarted resident catches up on each that came due while it was down. At or after that instant it writes one `room::timedout` or `room::lapsed`, guarded on the exact head it armed from: an answer, decline, cancel, disposition, or recovery that commits first leaves it nothing to write. A timeout never touches an item, a lapse never moves a result, neither changes a deadline, and a recovered item is armed again at its fresh expiry. |
| <a id="identity-required-question-evidence-is-explicit-and-assessed"></a>**Required question evidence is explicit and assessed** | A task's statement names the [question results](#identity-room-questions-separate-delivery-from-result-authority) it requires, and only an explicit `spice task require HANDLE --evidence RESULT` adds one, naming a result asked in that task's own room. Ordinary edits preserve the set; only the operator replaces (`--replace RESULT --with RESULT`) or waives (`--waive RESULT`) one, and replay refuses a statement whose set differs from those changes. While any required result is pending, missing, declined, cancelled, or timed out, `spice task done` and a clean review refuse with `task::evidence-unsatisfied` naming each result's state; a task requiring none is unaffected. Answered means available for assessment, never accepted: an ANS completes no review, phase, decision, or authorization. A retained candidate's evidence must be asked in its controller's room, and execution authorization requires each result answered before the latest certifying coverage, so a material change re-retains a candidate that needs fresh assessment. |
| <a id="identity-working-groups-are-one-guarded-partition-of-designs"></a>**Working groups are one guarded partition of designs** | Every working group lives in one journal partition: groups in order, each holding one to six Design tasks in order, no design in two groups, under one revision. The partition decides membership through the same grouping model as teams; only `group::move` (one design into an existing group, into a new group under a fresh identity, or out of every group) and `group::arrange` (a faithful restatement of group order, designs per group, and design order) change it, each a root fact witnessing the revision it read, so a stale revision refuses with `group::partition-moved`. A change commits under the one `group:partition` origin, except a move out of every group that [withdraws a held or coordinated Design](#work-working-group-standing-makes-a-design-active-or-passive), which commits under that Design's own task origin so its withdrawal cites the room's steering and claim. A move admits only a Design task, an arrangement never adds, drops, or repeats a design, and a group ends when its last design moves out. A group is labelled from its first design, never named. The board, room snapshot, and app catalog read this one partition. A group change touches no room head, task head, or execution authorization; it changes organization and [Design standing](#work-working-group-standing-makes-a-design-active-or-passive) only. |

## Steering

| Label | Invariant |
| --- | --- |
| <a id="steering-an-item-is-durable-before-delivery-is-attempted"></a>**An item is durable before delivery is attempted** | An item is accepted as one journal transaction before any delivery reader is notified. Every submission is its own item: identical text sent twice persists as two items, never silently merged. Submit cost does not grow with backlog depth. |
| <a id="steering-the-journal-inbox-is-authoritative"></a>**The journal inbox is authoritative** | The journal owns directive lifecycle history and current pending heads. Supervisors and the `inbox` command read it directly; no filesystem rendering participates in delivery or recovery. |
| <a id="steering-pending-steering-reaches-each-admitted-agent-boundary"></a>**Pending steering reaches each admitted agent boundary** | The command-driven supervisor reads the journal inbox when an occupant starts and on each resident kick, writes each changed rendering once, and interrupts an in-flight command or tool call for unheard direction. Each read runs the installed `spice` executable rather than the supervisor's own build, so a supervisor that outlives a resident restart onto a newer release still reads that resident's inbox; a read that fails delivers nothing and is retried by the next kick. For OMP, fresh operator direction uses steering admission: all queued messages retain publication order, reach the model after the current tool result, and precede its next tool call. The interrupted command remains a job; delivery does not stop it. Peer messages and background notices remain non-preemptive. A reminder restates a key only when its telling changed by arriving or climbing a stage; another key arriving or retiring restates nothing. After a compaction it replays pending steering with one recovery instruction; an empty inbox writes nothing. A key the occupant's visible prose or tool intent answered is withdrawn from every queued reminder, replay, and briefing as those words are decoded, and nothing names it again until the journal climbs its stage, which says the answer retired nothing. Without a running supervisor, delivery waits for the next occupant's start. |
| <a id="steering-delivery-expands-authenticated-quote-links"></a>**Steering delivery expands authenticated quote links** | Delivery replaces each quote link with its durable source text, recursively to a fixed depth; an unavailable or too-deep source becomes a one-line stub naming its target. The journal keeps the linked original, and every reread reconstructs the same delivery. |
| <a id="steering-a-quote-link-reads-as-its-source"></a>**A quote link reads as its source** | `spice quote TARGET`, and an omp occupant's `read` of a `spice://quote/v1` target, answer from the same resolution delivery uses: the recorded author, then the exact source text, with no side effect. An unresolvable target refuses with its stub; a write to any `spice://` URI other than a [task text](#work-a-task-text-write-replaces-only-the-text-its-writer-read) refuses, naming the index. A withdrawn read is never answered. An omp that refuses the scheme's registration runs on without it. |
| <a id="steering-acknowledgment-evidence-is-assistant-prose-on-the-lane-tape"></a>**Acknowledgment evidence is assistant prose on the lane tape** | An ACK or NAK header anywhere in authored assistant text, or in the intent a tool call declares, retires its key as the prose lands; no acknowledgment command exists. Its authority locus names the exact tape generation; retained steering evidence without that source refuses at writable open. Reasoning, tool arguments, code, quotes, rendered source, negation, hypotheticals, and narration about a header retire nothing. |
| <a id="steering-a-turn-ending-header-retires-at-occupant-exit"></a>**A turn-ending header retires at occupant exit** | When an occupant exits, every live key its final transcript answers retires under the same recognition rules, with the transcript locus as evidence, and no successor wakes to cite it. An unanswered key stays live for the lane's next occupant, including an MSG addressed to the occupant that left. |
| <a id="steering-reads-never-retire-an-item"></a>**Reads never retire an item** | A delivery read is not a disposition. `acked`, `declined` (a reasoned refusal, surfaced wherever acknowledgement is), and `canceled` are distinct terminal dispositions, and every live presentation stage admits each of them. |
| <a id="steering-a-lane-read-is-a-spice-resource"></a>**A lane read is a `spice://` resource** | An omp occupant's `read` of `spice://inbox`, `spice://lane`, and `spice://session/briefing`, `/turns`, `/sweep`, or `/context/SESSION` answers what `inbox`, `lane list`, and the matching `session` read render for that lane, under the occupant's own session identity and the grammar of [board reads](#work-a-board-read-is-a-spice-resource); a typed session page also names its continuation as a `spice://` URL. A retained supervisor reads briefings, resume notices, and resources through the executable image currently installed at its path, so a resident replacement also replaces the journal schema and protocol implementation those reads use; the resource image owns the seat's bounded graph continuation cache until another image replaces it. Reading acknowledges, retires, and redelivers nothing, however often omp reads or greps it. A live session's read of its own context refuses as the CLI does. |
| <a id="steering-only-a-serving-seat-is-taught-spice-urls"></a>**Only a seat that serves `spice://` is taught it** | An omp occupant's first turn waits for omp's answer to the scheme's registration. When omp accepts, that turn and each briefing after a compaction carry the scheme guide (the index, collections ending in member URLs, query flags, `continue:` URLs, line selectors, and that writes and authority acts stay `spice` commands), and the briefing names each quoted read a resource serves as its URL; a `spice://session/briefing` read does the same. A seat whose omp refused, or whose vendor has no host schemes, is never told and reads the commands it reads today, as does any read run in an occupant's shell. |
| <a id="steering-escalation-is-driven-by-silence-measured-from-publication"></a>**Escalation is driven by silence measured from publication** | Escalation is driven by silence measured from publication, on a stable lineage key, in three named stages before expiry. An operator resend keeps the key and re-presents the directive at the stage its silence has reached; it never advances presentation beyond that due stage. |
| <a id="steering-lapsed-items-remain-recoverable"></a>**Lapsed items remain recoverable** | A bounded lifetime moves unanswered intent to `lapsed`, which is never a decision. Recovery returns the same directive to live with its key, lineage, stage, and history intact. |
| <a id="steering-pending-inbox-terminal-wake-failure-deadletters-the-lane-s-pending-directives"></a>**PendingInbox terminal wake failure deadletters the lane's pending directives** | An automatic wake for pending steering that fails terminally to start an occupant moves every pending directive on that lane to `deadlettered`; other wake and delivery failures leave them live. A live peer message or reply whose lane leaves the residency dead-letters once with `steer::recipient-lane-removed`, so a lane that takes its place inherits nothing. Recovery restores a dead-lettered directive with its identity, lineage, and stage, but refuses a peer directive whose lane is gone. |
| <a id="steering-retired-direction-stays-readable-in-a-bounded-window"></a>**Retired direction stays readable in a bounded window** | An inbox read that asks for retirements also returns each visible lane's most recently retired directives, newest first, with their dispositions and retiring facts. A lane sees only its own. |
| <a id="steering-operator-direction-history-pages-whole-fanouts"></a>**Operator direction history pages whole fanouts** | An operator-only history read pages operator-authored directions as whole fanouts, newest first, each with every recipient's disposition. Filters keep or drop a fanout whole. Peer messages, replies, and resident-authored directives never appear. |
| <a id="steering-a-peer-message-is-attributed-to-its-sending-lane-and-carries-no-operator-authority"></a>**A peer message is attributed to its sending lane and carries no operator authority** | An authored [MSG](#steering-an-authored-msg-packet-admits-each-proved-recipient) is the only peer publication. Its delivery names the sending lane and the exact authoring occupant's address, or `unproved` when no single birth proves one, and frames the text as information that cannot override operator steering or task authority. It mints a key and retires, lapses, and quotes like any directive, but a pending peer message never wakes a lane; only operator steering does. |
| <a id="steering-an-answered-peer-message-replies-once-to-its-sender"></a>**An answered peer message replies once to its sender** | Retiring a live peer message journals, in the same write, one reply to the sending lane whose text is the answering header's clause. Each fanout recipient replies only for its own key. The reply addresses the sending lane rather than the occupant, so it is heard at that lane's next seat without waking it, and names the occupant whose session declared the answering transcript, or `unproved` when none did. A reply's own answer produces nothing further, and operator, resident, and taste direction never produce a reply. |
| <a id="steering-an-msg-packet-owns-exactly-its-body-hunk"></a>**An MSG, ASK, or ANS packet owns exactly its body hunk** | `MSG SELECTOR [SELECTOR ...]: BODY`, `ASK AUDIENCE[#TASK]: BODY`, and `ANS RESULT[#TASK]: BODY` are read by the acknowledgment header grammar with the same exclusions. An audience is an [occupant address](#identity-an-occupant-address-is-its-birth-position) or the literal `operator`. Each MSG selector is `AUDIENCE`, `AUDIENCE#TASK`, or `#TASK`: it names an audience, a task's room by its handle, or both, never a steering key, and it owns its own context rather than borrowing a neighbor's. An ASK names exactly one audience; an ANS names exactly the public result handle of the question it answers, and its optional context only asserts that question's stored context. A packet retires nothing by its header. The body runs from the colon to the next placed ACK, NAK, MSG, ASK, or ANS header or the end of the prose. The first defect, including a malformed handle, an audience named twice, one room-only context named twice, or an ASK or ANS naming other than one selector, refuses the whole packet; a refused packet never guesses or drops a route or borrows a neighbor's body. |
| <a id="steering-an-authored-msg-packet-admits-each-proved-recipient"></a>**An authored MSG packet admits each proved recipient** | A complete streamed assistant text block publishes a packet. When OMP settles an assistant message with no text block, a placed packet explicitly repeated in a tool call's intent is its fallback source through the same narrow seam as ACK/NAK; an intent beside visible text is suppressed as a caption. Reasoning, tool arguments, and ordinary tool-call intents never publish. Every recipient resolves against one residency. One guarded write delivers immediately to an address that view seats, or queues one unheard directive when the address proves a logical lane that view leaves vacant. The author's private notice distinguishes delivered, queued, and unavailable addresses; queued traffic wakes nobody. Once a successor is born, the predecessor address stays expired and its refusal names that successor's address even after the lane vacates; it never retargets. An unproved or removed address also receives and queues nothing. A packet that names the author's own address, or whose author has no proved birth, publishes nothing. A packet with no admitted route and no room records that refusal once and never later publishes. Message identity derives from the packet's transcript locus and exact addressed birth, so live, exit, and startup rereads publish it at most once. Once admitted, the directive is bound to its logical lane: through vacancy, start, resume, and renewal it keeps its key, body, and expiry, and whichever occupant next sits there hears and answers it, until the lane [leaves the residency](#steering-pending-inbox-terminal-wake-failure-deadletters-the-lane-s-pending-directives). |
| <a id="steering-an-ask-or-ans-publishes-with-its-result-in-one-write"></a>**An ASK or ANS publishes with its result in one write** | An ASK, an ANS, and an MSG naming `operator` also journal one `room::conversed` record in the same write as their directives, freezing the operator route, each admitted lane route with its context, and each room-only route. An ASK opens one pending [question result](#identity-room-questions-separate-delivery-from-result-authority) whose public handle is a freshly minted steering key; its lane directive carries that key whether immediately delivered or queued for a proved vacant lane. Any other unavailable lane audience publishes nothing. An operator route opens one pending operator item and wakes no lane. Only the question's named respondent lane answers, while its result is pending and before its deadline, and an asserted context must equal the stored one; any other ANS publishes nothing and its author is told why. The answering write settles the result, returns the body to a lane requester's seated occupant in the stored context or opens an operator item for an operator requester, and acknowledges the respondent's still-live ASK directive without a reply of its own. The respondent's NAK of that live directive declines the result instead. A conversation record's identity is its exact transcript hunk, so live, exit, and startup rereads publish it at most once, and the author's private notice names the result it opened or answered. |
| <a id="steering-an-msg-context-records-the-packet-in-its-room"></a>**An MSG context records the packet in its room** | Every `#TASK` context must name an existing task, or the whole packet refuses with no route published. In the same write as its directed messages, the packet records one `room::messaged` publication in each distinct room it names, naming its accounted transcript generation, event locus, the exact body byte range, its author occupant, and the directive each of its directed routes into that room opened, never the whole transcript. The journal admits that record only from the hunk's own lane and only when its directives are exactly the peer messages the same write opened into that room, in order, each sent from that lane by that author at that tape record. A directed `ADDRESS#TASK` message also freezes that room's birth stamp in its steering authority; the recipient's inbox row and `[PEER]` delivery header render the anchor's current task handle from that stamp. A missing anchor head refuses the read rather than dropping or guessing the context. That route also invites the addressed lane into the room: for the room's activity window after the newest `MSG` or `ASK` route that addressed it there, the lane listens to the room and receives its later publications through catch-up. An invitation makes no participant until the lane posts, and grants no claim, read permission, or wake. Recording is not receipt: the author hears one private recorded-in-room notice, no participant acknowledgment is implied, and nothing wakes. A packet records once per source locus and room, so rereads record nothing, while equal text at another locus is another publication. |
| <a id="steering-a-room-catch-up-cursor-advances-only-by-a-landed-delivery"></a>**A room catch-up cursor advances only by a landed delivery** | Each logical lane has at most one room catch-up cursor, a journal position across all of its rooms. Only a `room::delivered` fact moves it, naming the exact hunk where the delivery landed in an accounted transcript of that same lane and an earlier indexed room publication strictly past the current cursor; replay rebuilds it, and a renewed occupant continues it. Before any delivery, a lane's catch-up starts at its horizon: the position of the one `room::horizon` fact the resident records the first time it reconciles the occupied lane without a cursor. The journal refuses a horizon from any other writer, a second horizon, or one for a lane with a cursor; it claims no delivery or participation, and retained publications at or before it are never owed. Each room's source read begins strictly after that position, so older retained publications are neither decoded nor charged to the source bound; the target lane's current activity is recovered independently from its latest indexed publication. The next catch-up walks publications after the cursor, or else the horizon, in journal order under one item and byte budget shared across rooms, delivers what the lane is owed through the room history's source-backed presentation and exact continuation, and passes over its own packets, directed packets it already received, and rooms it does not currently listen to. It is queued as a private aside for the occupant's next safe boundary, never interrupts an in-flight command and never wakes a lane. Once the exact aside appears in that occupant's transcript, the driver keeps its replacement slot coalesced across intervening commits and compaction until `room::delivered` durably recognizes it; only an unheard, interrupted, or failed delivery retries. A failed landed-evidence write retains that exact observed aside in occupant memory and retries only the write, never its delivery. A changed cursor releases the landed page and admits its next continuation, while renewal cannot transfer occupant-scoped delivery state. Commit reconciliation projects only rooms indexed by a publication transaction and only the lane named by cursor progress; unrelated commits schedule no catch-up projection, while startup and task or residency authority changes retain complete reconciliation. |
| <a id="steering-an-authored-packet-presents-as-an-outgoing-quote"></a>**An authored packet presents as an outgoing quote** | In its author's Chat card, a well-formed MSG, ASK, or ANS renders its body as an outgoing quote when every addressed occupant proves a birth. The quote names the author and exact destinations without implying delivery; ASK carries a question badge, ANS carries an answer badge and the result it answers, and optional task context remains visible. The protocol header stays hidden. An unproved addressed occupant keeps the whole packet literal. Retained send annotations without a packet kind mean MSG. |
| <a id="steering-shell-instrumentation-is-one-shot-at-the-agent-s-own-command-boundary"></a>**Shell instrumentation is one-shot at the agent's own command boundary** | The wrapper receives each complete top-level agent command string exactly once and instruments nothing beneath it. The boundary is the process: a sourced script shares the immediate shell's functions, while an executed descendant gets the user's shell unmodified. Exactly-once is structural, distinct packaged environments rather than a counter, so there is no marker to read, forge, or lose. |
| <a id="steering-private-output-relays-end-with-the-immediate-child"></a>**Private output relays end with the immediate child** | When the immediate command process exits, the wrapper revokes inherited output relays and drains committed output, so a background descendant cannot hold it open. |

## Work, claims, and allocation

| Label | Invariant |
| --- | --- |
| <a id="work-at-most-one-active-claim-per-actor"></a>**At most one active claim per actor** | At most one active claim per actor. `task next` for an actor already holding one reports that task and its next action without entering allocation. |
| <a id="work-claiming-is-atomic"></a>**Claiming is atomic** | The win, active head, owner, and lease commit together, and every repository-backed claim, whether by allocation, explicit capture, or steal, records the holder's measured opening Git boundary. | <a id="work-an-explicit-steal-replaces-exactly-the-named-claim"></a>**An explicit steal replaces exactly the named claim** | An explicit steal is immediate and replaces exactly the named current claim or refuses. The displaced claimant learns through a typed ownership refusal on its next claim operation, not a fabricated steering item. |
| <a id="work-a-live-lease-rolls-with-its-occupant"></a>**A live lease rolls with its occupant** | The resident rolls a claim's lease while its lane seats the occupant holding it, so a lapse follows the occupant leaving rather than a fixed lease running out under long work. | <a id="work-lapse-writes-lapsed-before-readiness-is-derived"></a>**Lapse writes `lapsed` before readiness is derived** | Lapse and explicit free are typed journal transitions to `lapsed` and `freed`, so an intentional end is distinguishable from silence. Once written, the row is not peer-held and readiness is derived from every current predicate; before then the current claim stays authoritative. When the resident commits a lapse, the lane that held the claim receives one private notice at its current occupant, even one seated after the claim was won, naming the task and the expiry that passed; a vacant lane hears nothing, and the notice changes no claim, lease, or allocation state. |
| <a id="work-queue-age-has-one-durable-origin"></a>**Queue age has one durable origin** | Queue age has one durable origin, and unrelated row modifications do not reset it. |
| <a id="work-allocation-ranks-in-a-fixed-ladder"></a>**Allocation ranks in a fixed ladder** | Allocation ranks by phase-handoff preference, then last-resort class, then effective priority (a prerequisite inherits the highest priority it transitively unblocks), downstream weight, a narrow urgency band, peer spread, and locality. |
| <a id="work-agent-allocation-summaries-stop-at-the-design-authorization-boundary"></a>**Agent allocation summaries stop at the Design authorization boundary** | Automatic agent-facing board summaries omit Design roots and work still gated by a Design. Explicit board and Design reads retain the complete planning state throughout. |
| <a id="work-phase-handoffs-use-durable-transition-evidence"></a>**Phase handoffs use durable transition evidence** | A phase transition's workflow head and causal completion fact open a short ownership window without inspecting a phase name. | <a id="work-self-authored-review-is-ranked-last-never-excluded"></a>**Self-authored review is ranked last, never excluded** | The actor's own review, the prior independent-phase owner's row, and rows the actor freed rank last for that actor and are never excluded, so a quiet board hands an actor its own review rather than stalling. |
| <a id="work-locality-is-the-final-tie-break-and-never-overrides-the-graph"></a>**Locality is the final tie-break and never overrides the graph** | Only rows within a narrow urgency band of their group's top compare by locality. Among them a (project, phase) cell any peer on the board occupies ranks below a free one, then movement from the actor's most recently claimed cell costs one for a changed project and one for a changed phase. Crowding never excludes a row. |
| <a id="work-every-task-names-an-origin-realm"></a>**Every task names an origin realm** | Every origin has the shape `<class>:<value>` and records why the task exists, never who filed it. `ack:<key>` and `task:<born-stamp>` are the ordinary conventions; unknown classes are preserved rather than rejected. |
| <a id="work-a-task-flow-is-nonempty-unique-and-self-containing"></a>**A task flow is nonempty, unique, and self-containing** | A flow is a nonempty list of unique lowercase phase names containing the current phase. The board has no global phase vocabulary. |
| <a id="work-a-review-that-changes-nothing-still-advances-the-phase"></a>**A review that changes nothing still advances the phase** | A review that changes nothing still advances the phase, so absence of a commit is not absence of review. Review feedback reaches the phase author under the bare task key. |
| <a id="work-a-named-flow-outranks-the-review-omission-default"></a>**A named flow outranks the review-omission default** | A task created without a named flow omits its review phase when every reviewed parent or dependency is pending in review, because that parent's re-review is the closing check. A flow the author names is created exactly as written. |
| <a id="work-dependency-blocking-is-an-eligibility-predicate-not-a-task-transition"></a>**Dependency blocking is an eligibility predicate, not a task transition** | A pending task with an unsettled dependency stays pending but ineligible. A prerequisite settles when completed or deleted, without writing the dependent's workflow. Dependency edits are guarded publications that refuse an absent edge, a cycle, or a removal that leaves a plan relying on child acceptance without a pending accepted child. |
| <a id="work-working-board-cost-follows-live-and-reachable-work"></a>**Working-board cost follows live and reachable work** | Allocation, mutation, and open-board reads project the live graph plus only the settled tasks reachable from named or dependency edges. Explicit archive reads still project the retained history. |
| <a id="work-environmental-blocking-is-an-eligibility-predicate-not-a-task-transition"></a>**Environmental blocking is an eligibility predicate, not a task transition** | A task may require a minimum journal schema and one exact trust-grant digest. Until a current grant satisfies it the pending task is ineligible; a matching grant makes it eligible without rewriting or waking it. |
| <a id="work-plan-and-design-intent-is-declared-statement-authority"></a>**Plan and Design intent is declared statement authority** | `task plan` defaults to `plan,review`; `task design` gives a Design its one open `design` phase and refuses any other flow. Their purpose is statement authority, separate from the open phase vocabulary, and cannot be cleared or changed into another purpose. An undeclared task may acquire explicit intent unless it carries a preparation association: intent separates an associated preparation from a controlled output, so an associated task keeps the intent it was created with; it may declare Design only when its flow is exactly `design`. Phases never infer intent. Phase changes, review, deferral, dependency edits, and corrections retain the declaration. Journal verification checks intent against admitted facts and preparation against the immutable task birth. |
| <a id="work-preparation-is-explicit-and-provenance-is-not-a-gate"></a>**Preparation is explicit; provenance is not a gate** | Preparation records the immediate parent, controller, and construction owner by native task birth identity, atomically with creation. `--preparation HANDLE` binds explicitly. Without an explicit origin, ordinary creation under an ambient planning controller binds automatically. A Design is the planning of Plans; a Plan is the planning of executable tasks. A Plan is an ordinary reviewed task constructor: each acceptance-bearing task it constructs waits for that Plan to complete positively while its controller stands, open or concluded done, then becomes eligible on its ordinary dependencies with no candidate, decision, or authorization; a Plan a Plan constructs runs while its parent is open. Every output a Design constructs, normally a Plan, waits for that Design's [execution authorization](#work-execution-authorization-is-a-designs-exact-selection), so an unselected sibling stays pending; no ordinary edge to an open Design is added, because the Design stays open after selecting it. Explicit dependencies cannot remove this control gate. A controller that collapsed, closed, or was deleted releases nothing it controls. Corrections retain the original construction owner and carry its construction duty only while that owner is pending; once it settles, a correction completes as ordinary work. Origin alone creates no edge. Operator closure and deletion resolve ordinary prerequisites, but release no output and create no execution authorization. |
| <a id="work-execution-authorization-is-a-designs-exact-selection"></a>**Execution authorization is a Design's exact selection** | Candidates and exact decisions belong only to an open Design: `preparation retain` and `preparation decide` refuse any other controller. A Design whose work implies follow-up creates its acceptance-bearing output Plans under itself and retains an immutable candidate naming them. One operator-attributed decision names that exact candidate version and either selects a nonempty exact subset of its outputs, returns it with a nonempty reason, or requests collapse through the separate Design conclusion authority. A selected decision records its immutable decision and execution authorization atomically, witnesses every governed input and selected output against the retained candidate, releases exactly those outputs, and leaves the Design open. Later candidate versions and decisions may release other outputs without revising prior decisions; a stale candidate head, foreign output, changed input, duplicate decision, previously authorized output, or mismatched selection refuses. Coverage and task-review citations remain readable candidate evidence but never authorize or block a selection. Only an output the Design constructed directly is selectable; working-group moves organize Designs and grant no authority. `preparation retain`, `critique`, `cover`, and `decide` record canonical JSON authority, while `preparation adopt OUTPUT PREPARATION` can control only pending, unclaimed, uncontrolled ordinary work. A decision authorizes once, an output is authorized at most once, and no read, edit, dependency change, wake, release, capture, steal, closure, or deletion grants, moves, or drops authorization. Concluding a Design done refuses while any pending direct output remains unselected, naming the exact outputs and the decision or settlement repair. Collapse and delete leave every output pending and ineligible; returned and collapse-request decisions authorize nothing. `preparation show HANDLE` pages candidates, critiques, coverage, decisions, and authorization in immutable journal order. Schema 56 crosses a retained accepted decision only when its exact later authorization proves the selected subset, preserves a retained return with an explicit migration reason, strips coverage from authorization while retaining its evidence record, and refuses any candidate-less negative encoding with release `128e75e97`. |
| <a id="work-a-design-stays-open-until-the-operator-concludes-it"></a>**A Design stays open until the operator concludes it** | A Design is open while its workflow is pending with no completion; no lane step and no selection ends that. `task done` refuses a Design (`task::design-open`): its holder retains proposals and notes, checkpoints, and runs `spice task free`, and a Design never enters a review phase. Only `spice task conclude HANDLE --done` or `--collapse REASON`, an operator command witnessing the Design's statement, workflow, and claim, concludes it: done keeps a positive standing and requires every directly constructed output selected or settled; collapse needs a nonblank reason, settles negatively, and leaves its outputs gated. Concluding refuses a non-Design (`task::conclude-not-design`), an already concluded or withdrawn Design, a stale witness, and a Design any lane holds (`task::conclude-claimed`, directing the holder to checkpoint and free); it never takes work from a lane. `task close` refuses a Design. A concluded Design reads done when its completed workflow keeps a positive review, and collapsed when its review is closed, including a Design an earlier release completed by review, by selection, or by operator close; task detail shows the conclusion and a collapse's reason. Schema 55 crosses each pending Design of the `design,review` release once at open: its workflow moves onto the one open phase, unassigned and unclaimed in place, and each pending output it controls drops its ordinary edge to it; no fact is rewritten, and a retained head that does not decode refuses with that release named. |
| <a id="work-a-design-awaiting-selection-waits-on-the-operator-without-a-new-state"></a>**A Design awaiting selection waits on the operator without a new state** | An open Design with a pending output it constructed directly that no exact decision selects is ineligible for allocation, capture, recovery, and steal, and it starts no lane: a lane holding it gets no work-driven start, while pending steering still wakes that lane. `task status`, `board`, the empty-drain readout, and the browser count or name it as awaiting selection, not ready, review, or blocked. Selecting the exact output subset, or closing or deleting each unselected output, removes the wait with no lane action. A Plan never waits here, and an open Design with no unselected output keeps its ordinary eligibility. Candidate, critique, coverage, and decision records stay available without a claim. |
| <a id="work-working-group-standing-makes-a-design-active-or-passive"></a>**Working-group standing makes a Design active or passive** | The current working-group partition alone decides whether an open Design can be possessed. Outside every group it is passive: allocation never offers it and no lane is started for it; capture, steal, and recovery refuse `claim::design-passive`; and coordination refuses `room::design-passive`. A move that takes an unheld Design out of every group makes it passive in the same write. A move that takes out a Design a lane holds, in the same write, cancels its room's unanswered coordination and, unless a stop already stands, sends that holder lane one operator stop direction recorded as `room::shelved`. The direction is addressed to the seated occupant or, while the lane is vacant, to the birth address of the occupant that won the claim, so it queues for the lane's next seat; it tells the holder to checkpoint and run ordinary `spice task free`. The claim stays authoritative until that free parks its unpublished commits and dirty work; nothing is force-freed or discarded. The move witnesses the claim and coordination records it read, so a claim won meanwhile refuses it, and a claim or offer read before the move refuses after it. Moving a passive Design into a group makes it eligible again without touching its room, candidates, outputs, history, or authorization; repeated moves while a stop stands send nothing new. An arrangement cannot remove a Design, so it never deactivates one. |
| <a id="work-a-gather-task-collects-evidence-for-an-active-design"></a>**A gather task collects evidence for an active Design** | A gather task is an ordinary task whose one phase is `gather`, without declared intent, constructed directly under an open Design that stands in a working group; creation or filing refuses one under any other parent, including a passive Design, a Plan, or none (`task::gather-design`), and a concluded Design is no preparation parent at all. | <a id="work-a-taste-hold-is-visible-before-a-task-is-read"></a>**A taste hold is visible before a task is read** | A statement matching the taste policy holds its task in plan, and status, assignment, and refusal name the hold where a lane first meets it.
